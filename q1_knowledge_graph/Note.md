I separated the graph into case facts, shared domain knowledge, and conditions because they play different roles. The case layer records what was observed, the knowledge layer makes the officer's hypothesis explicit, and the conditions layer captures evidence that can weaken or strengthen it. The recommendation is computed by walking the shared chain and checking conditions rather than storing a fixed decision edge.

I used early invoice payment as an ambiguous signal. A documented discount can explain it, verified cash reserves weaken the thin-buffer hypothesis, and supplier-required prepayment can increase the need to review working capital. I left out probabilities, automatic credit scores and real personal data because there is no validated dataset to support them. The result is an explainable prompt for human investigation, not an automatic rejection rule.
